Activation Steering for Masked Diffusion Language Models

A lightweight representation-level control primitive for steering masked diffusion language models at inference time — using a single low-dimensional direction extracted from contrastive prompts.

What we found

Diffusion models expose a different geometry of control.

The work adapts activation steering to MDLMs and uses safety refusal as a deployment-relevant case study to study where, when, and how behavioral directions are accessible during diffusion.

1D

Low-dimensional refusal control

A single activation direction induces large, systematic shifts in refusal behavior across multiple masked diffusion models.

Pre

Pre-instruction access

Unlike causal autoregressive models, effective directions can be extracted from tokens before the user instruction.

Early

Timing matters

Steering has its strongest leverage at early denoising steps, with the earliest diffusion block contributing disproportionately.

跨

Language transfer

Directions transfer strongly between English and Chinese within a multilingual MDLM, but not reliably across architectures.

Diffusion-specific accessibility.

In an autoregressive transformer, earlier prompt tokens cannot attend to later instruction content. In an MDLM mask predictor, the full sequence is processed in parallel — making behavior-relevant information accessible at structurally earlier token positions.

Method

A single forward pass to extract; a global intervention to steer.

Direction extraction

For each candidate layer and structural token position, the method computes the mean residual activation for harmful and harmless prompt sets and takes the normalized difference:

v = (μharmful − μharmless) / ‖μharmful − μharmless‖₂

A single layer–token direction is selected on a held-out harmful validation set and reused for inference-time steering.

Direction application

The selected direction is applied through a projection-based residual-stream intervention during reverse diffusion. In the main setup, the same direction is used across all layers, token positions, and denoising steps.

No finetuning. No parameter update, no trajectory-level optimization, and no alteration of the underlying sampling algorithm are required.
Abstract

Paper abstract

Masked diffusion language models (MDLMs) generate text via iterative masked-token denoising, enabling mask-parallel decoding and distinct controllability and efficiency tradeoffs from autoregressive LLMs. Yet, efficient representation-level mechanisms for inference-time control in MDLMs remain largely unexplored. To address this gap, we introduce an activation steering primitive for MDLMs: we extract a single low-dimensional direction from contrastive prompt sets using one prompt-only forward pass, and apply a global intervention on residual-stream activations throughout reverse diffusion, without performing optimization or altering the diffusion sampling procedure. Using safety refusal as a deployment-relevant case study, we find that refusal behavior in multiple MDLMs is governed by a consistent, approximately one-dimensional activation subspace. Applying the corresponding direction yields large and systematic behavioral shifts and is substantially more effective than prompt-based and optimization-based baselines. We further uncover diffusion-specific accessibility: effective directions can be extracted not only from post-instruction tokens, but also from pre-instruction tokens that are typically ineffective in autoregressive models due to causal attention. Ablations localize maximal leverage to early denoising steps and mid-to-late transformer layers, with early diffusion blocks contributing disproportionately. Finally, in an MDLM trained on English and Chinese, extracted directions transfer strongly between English and Chinese, but do not reliably generalize to an autoregressive architecture, highlighting architecture-dependent representations of safety constraints.
