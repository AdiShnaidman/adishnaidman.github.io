01 / Contributions

Key contributions

We introduce a representation-level activation steering method for masked diffusion language models and characterize steering behavior across token position, denoising time, transformer depth, language, and model architecture.

01

Representation-level control for MDLMs

Existing diffusion-language-model control work largely operates at the sampling level. We adapt activation steering to masked diffusion, intervening directly on residual-stream representations during reverse diffusion.

02

Prompt-only direction extraction

The steering direction is estimated from contrastive prompts using a single unmasked forward pass. Extraction does not simulate the denoising trajectory, and deployment requires no gradient optimization or sampler modification.

03

Global application of a selected direction

A direction selected from one layer–token source is reused as a global intervention across layers, token positions, and reverse-diffusion steps, producing large and systematic behavioral shifts.

04

Pre-instruction steering is effective

Effective refusal directions can be extracted from structural tokens before the user instruction—positions that are normally causally uninformative in autoregressive models.

05

Where and when control matters

Ablations reveal a temporal and spatial asymmetry: leverage is strongest early in denoising, the earliest diffusion block matters disproportionately, and mid-to-late layers account for most of the effect.

06

Transfer across language and architecture

Steering transfers strongly English ↔ Chinese within the same MDLM, but fails to produce a measurable effect when transferred to an autoregressive model, pointing to architecture-dependent safety representations.

05 / Method

Direction extraction and intervention

01 / Extract

Contrastive prompt sets

Compute harmful and harmless mean residual activations at candidate layer–token positions from a single prompt-only forward pass.

02 / Select

One direction

Choose one normalized difference direction on a held-out harmful validation set.

03 / Apply

Global projection intervention

Reuse that direction across all layers, token positions, and reverse-diffusion steps during generation.

Direction extraction

For candidate layer ℓ and structural token position i, compute the normalized difference between harmful and harmless mean residual activations.

v = (μharmful − μharmless) / ‖μharmful − μharmless‖₂

Candidate token positions include stable chat-template markers both before and after the user instruction.

Direction application

During generation, hidden states are projected onto the subspace orthogonal to the selected direction. The same selected direction is used globally in the main setup.

No finetuning / no parameter update / no trajectory-level extraction / no modification of the underlying sampling algorithm
06 / Abstract

Paper abstract

Masked diffusion language models (MDLMs) generate text via iterative masked-token denoising, enabling mask-parallel decoding and distinct controllability and efficiency tradeoffs from autoregressive LLMs. Yet, efficient representation-level mechanisms for inference-time control in MDLMs remain largely unexplored. To address this gap, we introduce an activation steering primitive for MDLMs: we extract a single low-dimensional direction from contrastive prompt sets using one prompt-only forward pass, and apply a global intervention on residual-stream activations throughout reverse diffusion, without performing optimization or altering the diffusion sampling procedure. Using safety refusal as a deployment-relevant case study, we find that refusal behavior in multiple MDLMs is governed by a consistent, approximately one-dimensional activation subspace. Applying the corresponding direction yields large and systematic behavioral shifts and is substantially more effective than prompt-based and optimization-based baselines. We further uncover diffusion-specific accessibility: effective directions can be extracted not only from post-instruction tokens, but also from pre-instruction tokens that are typically ineffective in autoregressive models due to causal attention. Ablations localize maximal leverage to early denoising steps and mid-to-late transformer layers, with early diffusion blocks contributing disproportionately. Finally, in an MDLM trained on English and Chinese, extracted directions transfer strongly between English and Chinese, but do not reliably generalize to an autoregressive architecture, highlighting architecture-dependent representations of safety constraints.
