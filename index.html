Adi Shnaidman

I’m an AI researcher and MSE student in Computer and Information Science at the University of Pennsylvania, where I conduct research under the supervision of Prof. Dan Roth. My research focuses on reliable and trustworthy AI, with particular interests in LLM safety, robustness, interpretability, agent behavior, and inference-time control.

My recent work explores how model behavior is shaped by internal representations, prior interactions, and changing context, including questions around representation-level control, persistent effects in multi-turn interactions, and the reliability of adaptive AI agents.

More broadly, I’m interested in understanding why advanced AI systems behave the way they do, how undesirable or unexpected behaviors emerge and persist, and how deeper understanding of these processes can lead to systems that are more robust, reliable, and controllable.

Research Interests

My research broadly concerns the reliability and trustworthiness of advanced AI systems. I’m interested in how model behavior is shaped by internal computation, prior interactions, and deployment context, and in understanding when and why undesirable behaviors persist.

My work spans language-model behavior, mechanistic interpretability, representation-level model control, adversarial robustness, and the reliability of adaptive AI agents.

Research interests:

  • Reliable and trustworthy AI
  • LLM safety, robustness, and behavioral reliability
  • Mechanistic interpretability and representation-level model control
  • Multi-turn interaction dynamics and history dependence
  • AI-agent behavior and safety
  • Inference-time control and adversarial evaluation

Selected Research

Reset Is Not Recovery: Evaluating Recoverability from False Conversational Context via Sycophancy Hysteresis
A. Shnaidman
GroundLM Workshop at EMNLP 2026 · Accepted

Studies whether language models recover from false conversational context after user pressure is withdrawn, introducing sycophancy hysteresis and a hierarchy of recovery interventions.

Activation Steering for Masked Diffusion Language Models
A. Shnaidman, E. Feiglin, O. Yaari, E. Mentel, A. Levi, R. Lapid
ICLR 2026 ReALM-GEN Workshop

Introduces an inference-time activation steering method for masked diffusion language models, using activation-level interventions to steer model behavior during reverse diffusion.
